De-worming Guide

Why De-worming is Important for Pets

Internal parasites like worms are a common issue for pets. Regular de-worming is necessary to prevent these parasites from causing harm to your pet’s health. Worms can lead to various health complications, including digestive issues, weight loss, anemia, and even death in severe cases.

What are the Common Types of Worms?

The most common types of worms that affect pets include roundworms, tapeworms, hookworms, and whipworms. These worms can be transmitted through contaminated food, water, or soil. Puppies and kittens are especially susceptible to worm infestations.

How to De-worm Your Pet

De-worming involves giving your pet the appropriate medication that kills the worms. Your vet will recommend the best treatment based on your pet’s age, breed, and health condition. Usually, pets need to be de-wormed every month, but your vet might suggest more frequent treatments if your pet is at higher risk.

When to Start De-worming

Start de-worming your puppy or kitten at 2 weeks old. After the initial treatment, repeat the de-worming every two weeks until they are 12 weeks old. Once your pet reaches 12 weeks, de-worming should be done every 3 months for adult pets.

Signs Your Pet Might Need De-worming

  • Visible worms in feces or vomit
  • Weight loss despite a normal appetite
  • Vomiting and diarrhea
  • Bloated abdomen

Consult Your Veterinarian

It's important to consult your veterinarian before starting any de-worming regimen. They can guide you on the best de-worming schedule and products for your pet’s specific needs.
